# Make and control consistent material

A specification is a decision rule backed by a measurement and a rationale. It is not a substitute for knowing how a product is made, what could vary, or whether the measurement can reveal that variation. The FDA cases reviewed here show three recurring failures of connection: a proposed limit is not related to patient exposure; a method cannot support the conclusion drawn from it; or a manufacturing change is not connected to the material used to establish safety and benefit.

This review examines eleven programs, nine involving small molecules and two antibody comparators. The public evidence is strongest for impurity qualification, formulation-dependent tests and change assessment. It is much thinner for complete manufacturing processes, sampling plans and operating ranges, which are often redacted. The lesson should use the visible decisions to explain control strategy without supplying an imaginary factory behind the black boxes.

## Early controls answer a bounded safety question

Glycopyrrolate inhalation provides a genuine initial IND example. During the 30-day review, FDA asked the sponsor to clarify related-substance limits and provide assay and impurity profiles before and after nebulization. The subsequent memo noted that observed levels in clinical lots were considerably below the proposed limits and concluded that no additional nonclinical impurity qualification was needed for the proposed 14-day Phase 2 study. It separately discussed future NDA or longer-duration studies. This initial IND did not start with a Phase 1 study, a useful reminder that submission stage and clinical phase are different descriptors. [Glycopyrrolate, original IND 110663 memo, PDF 70, 75](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2017/208437Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=70).

The scientific lesson is the joint assessment of actual material, permitted variability, dose and duration. A lot result below a limit establishes something about that lot; it does not automatically justify allowing every future lot to approach the limit. Conversely, a short clinical study need not wait for every specification that will eventually support chronic commercial use. The numerical limits are largely redacted. The memo's historical classification of benzoic acid also differs from the later review, so neither its terminology nor historical thresholds should become current teaching rules.

Vamorolone supplies a contrasting initial IND outcome. Its December 2014 submission was placed on partial clinical hold in January 2015 because two drug-substance impurities had structural alerts for genotoxicity. Submission of completed genotoxicity studies was followed by removal of the hold in March. This is a concrete instance in which uncertainty about what accompanied the active substance altered clinical development. The public regulatory history does not disclose enough here to reconstruct impurity identities, test results or numerical limits; removal of the hold should not be paraphrased as proof that every alert was a false positive. [Vamorolone, initial IND 118942 history, PDF 116](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/215239Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=116).

Together these cases avoid two simplistic lessons: impurities are neither a paperwork detail nor an automatic demand for a fixed animal battery. The team must identify the uncertainty, relate it to the proposed exposure, and provide evidence or controls adequate for that question.

## A limit means little until translated into use

Baricitinib's end-of-Phase-2 interaction asked for structures of impurities and intermediates, monitoring of impurities and degradation products, and qualification when applicable during IND development and for an NDA. The important feature is the continuing connection between process knowledge and the safety assessment. The passage is later IND advice; its citation to historical draft genotoxicity guidance should not be used as a current numerical standard. [Baricitinib, EOP2 history, PDF 53–54](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2018/207924Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=53).

SoluPrep makes the exposure calculation explicit. In an NDA complete response, FDA found the rabbit dermal study inadequate to support proposed impurity specifications for maximal use of the 26 mL applicator. Tissue assessment and exposure support were insufficient. FDA offered routes involving adequate qualification or exposure data, or tighter limits tied to the total daily exposure from a relevant approved product. Four applications per day mattered. The inference is that “percentage impurity” must be translated through amount applied, frequency and relevant absorption before it can support a safety conclusion. The letter does not mandate the same maximal-use or animal study for every topical product. [SoluPrep, NDA complete response, PDF 2–3](https://download.open.fda.gov/crl/208288_2018_Orig1s000OtherActionLtrs.pdf#page=2).

A glycopyrrolate prefilled syringe shows the same logic for endotoxin. FDA's NDA filing correspondence questioned the method's maximum valid dilution and calculated whether the specification could exceed the permitted hourly exposure at the proposed adult and pediatric dosing frequencies. The limit had to agree with both method capability and maximum clinical use. These were additional comments, explicitly separate from the refusal-to-file grounds. They are a useful later-stage worked example, not an initial IND checklist or a universal concentration limit. [Glycopyrrolate injection, NDA 214919, PDF 7](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/214919Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=7).

CyclASol, the cyclosporine ophthalmic program, adds a subtler point. At its pre-NDA meeting, sponsor and FDA agreed on a numerical unspecified-impurity limit, but FDA attributed acceptability to the low ophthalmic product strength rather than the sponsor's proposed total-daily-intake reasoning. Agreement on a number is not agreement on the argument. The lesson should preserve the basis because that basis determines whether the limit remains defensible after a strength, route or dose change. This historical product-specific discussion is not a general threshold recommendation. [CyclASol, March 2022 meeting under IND 128163, PDF 7–8](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/217469Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=7).

## Give each test a job, then establish that it can do it

ACER-001 sodium phenylbutyrate is an especially useful counterexample to “more tests means better control.” FDA wanted a discriminating dissolution method and multi-timepoint data. When the sponsor clarified that taste-mask coating integrity testing was exploratory, FDA said it was not a required component of routine QC; taste panels would better inform patient-acceptability claims. Adequate coating controls were still expected. The decision distinguishes understanding the formulation, evaluating a patient experience and releasing routine batches. One test should not be assumed to answer all three. [ACER-001, pre-NDA IND meeting, PDF 7–8](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/214860Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=7).

Sesquient fosphenytoin demonstrates why the assay's purpose matters. FDA found that inadequate validation of the HPLC method for degradation products prevented assessment of the stability data and establishment of shelf life. A later review recorded revalidation and acceptance of a shorter shelf life, while an independent particulate issue remained unresolved at that stage. A method described as validated for some purposes had not thereby established accuracy for the relevant impurities. This is NDA evidence; the document's references to earlier pre-IND discussions do not relocate these deficiencies to the original IND. [Sesquient, NDA quality reviews, PDF 22, 34](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/210864Orig1s000ChemR.pdf#page=22).

The same review examined competing explanations for foreign particles. A second laboratory's different findings did not, without supporting evidence, establish that the first result was merely laboratory contamination. FDA asked for characterization, root cause, mitigation and evidence preventing recurrence. This supports a decision-oriented investigation exercise: what observation would distinguish contamination during manufacture, interaction with the container and contamination during testing? Repeating a measurement is useful only if the repeat helps resolve those alternatives. Later pages address subsequent review; the cited 2019 assessment is not the final outcome of the program. [Sesquient, PDF 5, 22](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/210864Orig1s000ChemR.pdf#page=5).

The syringe glycopyrrolate refusal-to-file adds a related evidentiary point. Chromatograms without interpretable methods, sample preparation and quantitative results did not allow substantive leachables review. Additional comments questioned a no-detect result in light of observed extractables, missing validation and testing at only one timepoint. “Nothing detected” is a conclusion whose strength depends on what the analysis could detect. It should not become an unconditional statement that nothing is present. [Glycopyrrolate injection, NDA filing letter, PDF 2, 5](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/214919Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=2).

## Consistency includes continuity with the evidence

Osimertinib connects process change to a concrete safety comparison. An NDA-reviewed one-month rat study compared two batches produced by different manufacturing processes for impurity qualification. One batch had been used in the toxicology study submitted at IND filing. The reviewer reported no apparent worsening of toxicity and no meaningful toxicokinetic exposure difference. The inference is narrow: this comparison helped interpret these materials under these conditions. The redacted impurity table cannot support a reconstructed acceptable impurity profile or a generic purity cutoff. [Osimertinib, NDA impurity-qualification study, PDF 121–122](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2015/208065Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=121).

Teplizumab, an antibody comparator, shows what a prospective change plan needs. FDA did not accept a draft outline of comparability following a planned manufacturing transfer; it requested a detailed protocol and suggested further CMC discussion when preliminary process data and a finalized plan were available. The decision was about whether the proposed evaluation could establish continuity, with final manufacturing acceptability reserved for license review. An outline naming future work was not itself that evidence. [Teplizumab, 2018 EOP2 IND minutes, PDF 312–313](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2026/761183Orig1s010.pdf#page=312).

Dupilumab shows the clinical interface. A stability issue led to a manufacturing change during an asthma trial. FDA recommended an efficacy sensitivity analysis comparing material exposures; the reported 374-patient subset showed no noticeable difference from the overall efficacy estimates. This does not prove analytical equivalence, but it shows why a quality change may require checking how clinical evidence is pooled. The contaminant and removed constituent are redacted, and this was a supplemental BLA review. [Dupilumab, asthma supplement, PDF 61](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2020/761055Orig1s007.pdf#page=61).

## Regulatory floor and lesson construction

Most Phase 1 investigational drugs are exempt from 21 CFR Part 211 under 210.2(c), while remaining subject to statutory CGMP. The exemption excludes drugs already made available for Phase 2 or Phase 3 use, or lawfully marketed drugs, even when subsequently used in Phase 1. FDA recommends appropriate QC responsibility and reliable laboratory testing. This does not mean commercial process validation is the universal starting point, or that early material may be uncontrolled. [21 CFR 210.2(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-210/section-210.2); [Phase 1 CGMP guidance, PDF 4, 7, 10, 13](https://www.fda.gov/media/70975/download#page=4).

FDA's Phase 2/3 CMC guidance distinguishes safety-related information from corroborating development information and encourages preservation of knowledge needed to connect clinical and future commercial material. Its scope excludes proteins and other biologics. Guidance recommendations and individual reviewer requests should remain distinguishable from binding regulation and from our proposed teaching exercises. [Phase 2/3 CMC guidance, PDF 4–7](https://www.fda.gov/media/70822/download#page=4).

The lesson should end with a control rationale for one defined clinical product: attribute or failure mode; how it could arise; measurement or preventive control; proposed acceptance rule; basis in data and patient use; response when the rule is not met. Students should be able to remove an uninformative test as confidently as they add a necessary one. The handoff to nonclinical work identifies which impurity or changed material needs qualification. The handoff to clinical work supplies lot identity and change history. Stability then tests whether those controls continue to support suitable material through the last administration.
