# Identify and interpret potential harm

*A focused review of public FDA development records for lesson construction · September 17, 2026*

**The useful output of nonclinical safety work is a reasoned account of potential harm: what happened, under what conditions, how confidently it is attributed to treatment, and what it implies for people.** The records resist a simple pass/fail model. They also resist a more sophisticated-looking shortcut in which every animal finding is dismissed once a plausible mechanism is proposed.

Ten programs are examined here, primarily small molecules with faricimab as an antibody contrast. Sources include retained initial-IND findings, later IND assessments and explicitly labeled marketing-review interpretations. Critical passages were reread; this is a purposive review rather than an exhaustive census. PDF references use file pages. The matching JSON separates source facts from lesson interpretations, and the audit identifies what was actually checked. These historical cases teach reasoning; they are not current treatment instructions.

Manual scope for this review: **13 PDFs and 29 document-pages** checked across retained cases and additional triage; **10 programs** retained in detail. The frozen automated queue contains 557 candidate PDFs and 1270 candidate pages; it was not reviewed exhaustively. Some earlier verified PDFs lie outside that queue.

## 1. Begin with an observation that another reader could reconstruct

**Ivacaftor’s initial IND safety pharmacology** did not produce one undifferentiated “negative battery.” Its hERG experiment was limited by solubility and included vehicle and positive controls. In-vivo telemetry assessed different endpoints and conditions, while rat assays showed gastrointestinal effects. The absence of an adverse ECG signal did not make the GI findings disappear, nor did it establish an unlimited negative hERG result. [FDA review, PDF 87–89](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2012/203188Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=87).

The first lesson action should be to rewrite a conclusion such as “cardiac safety was acceptable” into the observations that support it. Which system was tested? At what concentration or exposure? For how long? With what controls and sensitivity? This is not an invitation to catalogue every laboratory detail. It identifies the details that bound a negative result. A negative assay is useful precisely because its tested conditions are known.

**Cipepofol’s marketing-stage QT consult** makes this especially vivid. Its summary juxtaposes a large in-vitro margin with a dog QTc finding at a much smaller exposure multiple, while noting possible confounding by increased heart rate during injection. Human thorough-QT and clinical observations were considered separately. [FDA consult, PDF 146](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2026/220482Orig1s000OtherR.pdf#page=146).

That page does not resolve the confounder or establish human proarrhythmia. It does show why an integrated assessment cannot be replaced by choosing the largest reassuring ratio. This should be a later-development comparison exercise, not an initial-IND case: place each experiment, its signal and its interpretive limitation in one table, then identify what explanation is still missing.

## 2. Reversibility is a claim about a trajectory

In **osimertinib’s one-month dog study**, gastrointestinal toxicity led to a dosing interruption and reduction. Corneal findings partially regressed after that change, but opacity was not fully reversible in recovery animals. The study had small groups and no vehicle-control recovery cohort. There were no preterm deaths, which was a different observation rather than a substitute for these findings. [FDA study assessment, PDF 142, 144–145](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2015/208065Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=142).

The lesson should show onset, interruption, reduced treatment and post-treatment observation on a single timeline. Improvement during lower exposure, resolution after stopping, and persistence at the end of observation support different claims. They also raise different questions: whether the lesion stabilizes, whether delayed effects emerge, and whether the recovery window is long enough. The public appendix does not establish this study as the entire original IND or prescribe the eventual clinical monitoring schedule.

This distinction matters beyond terminology. If a learner labels the lesion “reversible,” the proposed risk plan may implicitly assume recovery that the study did not demonstrate. A more accurate statement preserves both information and uncertainty: partial regression was observed under specified dose changes, while a residual finding remained at the last assessment. That wording is scientifically more useful than a green or red badge.

## 3. A mechanism can inform human relevance without settling it

**Faricimab’s retained initial review** reports severe ocular inflammation. The sponsor attributed animal toxicity to anti-drug antibodies, but the reviewer found that evidence supporting that explanation elsewhere did not establish it in the eye. The lower proposed clinical dose was supported; the higher dose was not, and lower-dose human information was to be assessed before escalation. [Retained initial findings, PDF 26; development history, PDF 15](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761235Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=26).

This is a strong case for separating observation, causal explanation and relevance judgment. “Animals can develop antibodies to human proteins” is general knowledge. “These antibodies caused this ocular lesion and therefore it will not occur in people” requires additional evidence. The review left that causal uncertainty open and changed the clinical plan. It did not demand that every mechanistic question be completely resolved before any investigation could proceed. Its internally awkward chronology should not be reconstructed into a more precise history than the public document supports.

**Rilzabrutinib’s record** adds a particularly instructive experiment. Its history recounts an initial IND hold involving animal neuroinflammation. A separate mechanistic assessment compares oral and intravenous dosing: the negative IV result occurred at substantially lower exposure, which the reviewer considered potentially insufficient to test the route hypothesis. Intrajejunal dosing reduced CNS findings at more comparable plasma exposures, but the implications remained unclear. [Initial history, PDF 101; mechanism studies, PDF 112–113](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/219685Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=101).

This does not establish which experiments lifted the hold. It teaches the design needed to make a causal claim: if route and exposure both change, the experiment cannot cleanly attribute the difference to route. A more discriminating result can narrow uncertainty without completing the explanation.

**Tenapanor** offers a small-molecule counterpart. Rat and dog tolerability differed markedly, principally through intestinal fluid retention and diarrhea. The sponsor compared expression of relevant intestinal transporters across species. FDA questioned how much weight to give that comparison because sampling standardization and differences in maturity were inadequately described. [Later IND-linked NDA assessment, PDF 168–169](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/213931Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf#page=168).

An expression result can be biologically plausible and still be methodologically insufficient to dismiss a sensitive species. The next useful experiment would discriminate the pharmacological explanation from collection or developmental-stage artifacts. The cited record does not identify a universally “correct” species, nor does it prove the sponsor’s explanation false. It shows a claim that remained less secure than its narrative suggested.

## 4. Target-related actions can create risk

**Tovorafenib’s NDA assessment** describes pathway activation rather than suppression in NF1-loss-of-function cells at clinically relevant concentrations. An NF1 animal model showed no tumor reduction, with a nonsignificant increase in a minority of animals. The clinical and nonclinical teams agreed to a precaution concerning possible tumor growth in NF1 tumors. [FDA review, PDF 46–47](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/217700Orig1s000,%20218033Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf#page=46).

This is a particularly useful corrective to the idea that safety is primarily an off-target screening exercise. The same pathway-directed candidate can behave differently in another molecular context. At the same time, the evidence must not be inflated: functional activation, a nonsignificant animal tumor observation and a precautionary human conclusion are not interchangeable levels of proof. This is marketing-review reasoning, not documented original-IND content.

The lesson’s harm map should therefore include excessive intended action, altered action in a different biological context, off-target effects, metabolites and product/route-related effects. Those categories are prompts for inquiry, not compulsory boxes to fill. A learner should be able to explain why a particular concern belongs in the next clinical investigation rather than recite a generic list of possible mechanisms.

## 5. Monitorability changes the decision without making an adverse effect non-adverse

**Baricitinib’s 2009 later-IND memo** distinguished low no-adverse-effect levels from higher exposures associated with reversible, clinically monitorable effects. It considered chronic rat and dog findings alongside the proposed patient trial and available human experience. The reviewer supported a defined trial extension while separately identifying additional support needed for a chronic marketing indication. [Actual IND memo, PDF 248–249](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2018/207924Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=248).

The important comparison is not “toxicity present versus absent.” It is severity, exposure, reversibility, ability to detect change and the consequences of acting on that change in the proposed setting. The memo’s LOAEL-based reasoning must remain historical and case-specific. It does not authorize routine dosing at adverse-effect exposures, and a monitorable laboratory abnormality does not make every downstream consequence readily manageable.

A good learner exercise would require a short argument for each proposed precaution: which animal observation motivates it, whether the relevant human measurement is timely and interpretable, and what action follows a change. That becomes a handoff to clinical design. It should not invent a monitoring schedule that the source never recommended.

## 6. Human observations should revise the nonclinical risk account

**Lumateperone’s later development** retained concern about aniline metabolites and described exposure limitations and repeated metabolite assessments during an extension. Later human studies, including selected metabolic-inhibitor conditions, found the two metabolites associated with dog neuropathology below the quantification limit. [Development history, PDF 58–59; subsequent measurements, PDF 67](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/209500Orig1s005_Redacted.pdf#page=58).

This sequence demonstrates a focused update. It reduces uncertainty about the specified metabolites under tested conditions without establishing zero exposure or resolving every possible population and interaction. The historical restriction uses NOEL terminology and does not fully specify every element needed to reconstruct a threshold. It should not be silently converted into a generic numerical safety rule. The useful story is concern, targeted measurement, revised confidence and remaining boundary.

**Venetoclax** supplies the opposite direction of update. The clinical review says the original starting-dose proposal drew on mouse and dog pharmacology/toxicology. Early tumor lysis and subsequent severe events led to changes in starting dose, ramp-up, risk stratification and prophylaxis. [FDA clinical review, PDF 64, 96](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2016/208573Orig1s000MedR.pdf#page=64).

The source does not reproduce the underlying animal calculation or establish which nonclinical model predicted or missed the syndrome. It should not be used as a retrospective accusation against an unspecified experiment. Its value is that initial support never ends safety learning. New human observations can change the relevant risk model, including how disease burden and dosing sequence enter that model. The clinical-dose lesson can handle the resulting regimen; this lesson should explain why the original risk interpretation had to evolve.

## Regulatory expectations and scientific judgment

The IND regulation calls for an integrated toxicology summary and information adequate to support the proposed investigation, together with safety information as development proceeds. It does not promise a risk-free experiment or require every possible mechanism to be resolved. [21 CFR 312.23(a)(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.23).

ICH S7A recommends selecting safety-pharmacology studies around the candidate, intended use, relevant systems and prior findings. It recognizes that functional effects may not be detected by conventional toxicology observations and that measurements need appropriate timing, sensitivity and controls. The guidance is nonbinding and encourages scientifically valid approaches; it is not a requirement for a disconnected collection of standard assays. [FDA ICH S7A, PDF 5–7](https://www.fda.gov/media/72033/download#page=5).

The sponsor must still judge what the evidence means and which uncertainty could change the next action. The historical examples do not establish fixed rules about recovery animals, species or study duration. Those package-design questions belong in the next lesson, informed by the specific concern rather than by an unqualified checklist.

## Implications for constructing this lesson

Use one evolving **finding-to-risk table**: observation; dose/exposure and timing; attribution; severity and reversibility; human-relevance argument; unresolved question; proposed implication for the trial. Keep source observations and the learner’s interpretation visibly separate.

Faricimab can anchor a decision under unresolved causality, with osimertinib testing the recovery claim and tenapanor testing a species explanation. Baricitinib shows how a defined later trial may proceed despite characterized hazards. The final output should be a concise risk account with justified precautions and further questions. “Build a credible safety package” then asks whether the studies and records are adequate to support that account; it should not repeat the hazard catalogue.
