# The first clinical study: turning a development rationale into a controlled learning plan

Prepared 17 September 2026 for future lesson construction. This is a focused review of public FDA material, not a protocol template or a claim to have reconstructed complete IND applications. Twelve programs provide contrasts across oncology, infection, ophthalmology, metabolic disease, and inherited disease. Small molecules provide the main drug-development examples; antibodies and replacement enzymes serve as explicitly different biological comparators. The accompanying evidence file distinguishes original correspondence from retrospective summaries and preserves one-based PDF page references.

## The decision this lesson should teach

After selecting a candidate, the team must specify what it will learn from exposing a particular group of people to a particular regimen. A favorable nonclinical assessment does not itself supply the population, measurements, observation period, or rules for responding to an unexpected finding. The protocol makes these choices operational. Its quality depends on connecting the proposed scientific question with the evidence already available and the uncertainty that remains.

The legal framework is more flexible than a universal “Phase 1 checklist” suggests. Under 21 CFR 312.21, Phase 1 can involve patients or healthy volunteers and includes learning about human pharmacology, metabolism, increasing-dose effects, and sometimes early activity. Under 312.23(a)(6), a Phase 1 protocol can be less detailed than a later-phase protocol, but safety-critical elements require detail. Objectives, selection criteria, dosing and exposure duration, measurements, and risk-minimizing procedures remain central. These are regulatory requirements; a particular cohort size, escalation algorithm, biomarker, or stopping threshold is a program-specific design choice that needs justification. [21 CFR 312.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.21); [21 CFR 312.23(a)(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.23).

A useful distinction is “first human study” versus “first study under this IND.” An initial US IND may arrive after foreign clinical experience, may concern an additional indication, or may contain a Phase 2 protocol. The website should identify the actual milestone rather than attach every IND event to a fictional common starting line.

## Begin with a question and a population

**Oteseconazole** gives a relatively clear healthy-volunteer starting point. Its 2011 initial IND contained a randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled study of single oral doses in healthy adults, directed at safety, tolerability, and PK. A later Phase 2a study was explicitly considered exploratory, requiring stronger statistical testing for a definitive trial. The lesson is not that every drug needs this exact sequence. It is that the first study can answer a limited human-exposure question without pretending to establish clinical benefit. [FDA integrated review, PDF 127](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/215888Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=127).

**Ensartinib** shows how a disease hypothesis shapes early patient enrollment. FDA's recorded pre-IND advice asked the sponsor to specify accrual of patients whose tumors lacked both ALK and cMET alterations, because activity was hypothesized in tumors with those alterations. The initial dose-escalation study enrolled advanced solid tumors; expansion focused on ALK-positive NSCLC. FDA also asked for sampling capable of characterizing single- and multiple-dose PK, potential PD endpoints, and major cardiac safety signals. Thus, enrollment, sampling, and safety surveillance were connected to the same scientific rationale. This is a 2014 IND meeting record recounting 2011 advice and the 2012 opening study, not the original protocol itself. [FDA correspondence, PDF 49–50](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/218171Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=49).

Population eligibility can also change after entry. For **evinacumab**, emerging rabbit embryo-fetal findings led to a partial hold affecting women of childbearing potential; submitted nonclinical and clinical information plus consent revisions preceded removal. A later adolescent hold concerned inadequately assessed neurodevelopmental risk. This is evidence of population-specific reconsideration during IND development, not a reason to exclude all such populations automatically. [FDA IND correspondence, PDF 4–5](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/761181Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=4).

## Make measurements capable of answering the question

Permission to investigate and agreement that a design can establish efficacy are different judgments. **Levacetylleucine** makes this distinction unusually visible. At pre-IND discussion, FDA considered an open-label study potentially useful for proof of concept but inadequate for interpretable marketing-application efficacy evidence. During initial IND review, the sponsor acknowledged the exploratory purpose, removed an extension, and limited exposure to six weeks. FDA then issued a study-may-proceed letter while recommending further discussion of randomization, outcome measures, and statistics. The appropriate lesson is “define what this study can conclude,” rather than equating permission with endorsement of an eventual approval strategy. [FDA integrated review, PDF 140](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/219132Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=140).

**Paltusotine** illustrates a different limitation: carryover from prior therapy can obscure the drug's effect. Its initial US IND contained Phase 2 trials, which FDA considered safe to proceed while expressing design concerns. The agency recommended placebo-controlled dose-ranging and a washout to reduce confounding from prior somatostatin-analog therapy. Later, it judged the available studies insufficient to establish the dose-response relationship needed for the proposed Phase 3 program, including uncertainty about residual treatment effects. This is not a first-ever-human dosing case. It is an instructive warning that measuring a plausible biomarker does not by itself produce an interpretable drug-response experiment. [FDA integrated review, PDF 150–151](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/219070Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=150).

For **pegzilarginase**, later IND discussions distinguished rapid biochemical change from functional improvement. FDA requested a defined plasma-arginine metric and explained that a period sufficient to observe arginine reduction might be too short to assess motor or mobility changes. Baseline functional deficits and the ability to perform assessments also needed definition. These are later development decisions; their relevance to the first-study lesson is prospective: choose measurements and duration that match the claim, and state which questions remain for later trials. [FDA meeting record, PDF 25,27–28](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2026/761211Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=25).

## Convert risk into operating rules

**Faricimab** demonstrates why “monitor safety” is too vague. The reproduced initial IND assessment supported the proposed starting dose but not the highest dose because severe ocular inflammation remained unresolved. FDA and the sponsor agreed that lower-dose human data would be reviewed before higher doses were administered. The practical output was a condition on progression between cohorts. Uncertainty about whether animal inflammation reflected direct toxicity or anti-drug antibodies was not simply dismissed. The source contains inconsistent calendar entries, so precise submission chronology should not be reconstructed from this summary alone. [FDA pharmacology review, PDF 15,26](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761235Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=15).

**Risdiplam** supplies a broader operating example. Pre-IND advice recognized that monitoring might not necessarily prevent irreversible retinal injury, yet recommended surveillance in light of the serious disease and potential benefit. The infant study used staggered dosing, PK-based reassessment, an internal monitoring committee and an independent committee, with specialist review of ophthalmic assessments. Monitoring therefore had a defined purpose and decision-maker; it did not transform an uncertain hazard into a guaranteed preventable one. These passages concern patient development after a healthy-adult single-dose study, not the molecule's very first exposure. [FDA clinical review, PDF 24–25,72–74](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2020/213535Orig1s000MedR.pdf#page=24).

For **glofitamab**, a later IND meeting linked cytokine-release experience directly to observation arrangements: FDA required hospitalization for the second dose in patients who experienced any-grade cytokine release syndrome with the first dose, while encouraging further dose optimization. This is a historical protocol decision, not a current prescribing instruction. It shows how an observed event can trigger a specified consequence for subsequent treatment. [FDA multidisciplinary review, PDF 36–37](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/761309Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf#page=36).

**Venetoclax** is the strongest example of learning changing the operating plan. Tumor lysis after early doses and later fatal events led to changes in starting dose, ramp-up, tumor-burden stratification, prophylaxis, hospitalization, and laboratory surveillance. The clinical review traces these amendments and the partial-hold history. The response was a coordinated redesign, so improvement cannot be attributed to a lower number alone. The website should teach the sequence “signal → interpretation → amended protection → controlled restart,” without presenting historical regimens as instructions for treating patients. [FDA clinical review, PDF 28,64,96](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2016/208573Orig1s000MedR.pdf#page=96).

## Progression is conditional, even inside one protocol

**Olipudase alfa** shows explicit boundaries around permission. An initial full hold reflected insufficient preclinical and CMC information. Additional information supported a single-dose adult study; later repeat-dose investigation was allowed only at limited dose, interval, and duration while other investigations remained on hold. Human inflammatory reactions then contributed to a gradual within-patient escalation strategy. The program did not cross one permanent “safe for humans” threshold; the acceptable investigation widened as evidence accumulated. [FDA integrated review, PDF 52–53,132,163](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761261Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=163).

Likewise, **futibatinib's** initial combined Phase 1/2 protocol had a partial hold on the Phase 2 portion. Later safety data, preliminary activity, expansion-dose justification, and responses to product-quality issues supported hold removal. A combined phase label did not eliminate the decision boundary. [FDA multidisciplinary review, PDF 35,37](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/214801Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf#page=35).

These cases should not imply FDA normally holds every program. Public approval packages are selected, redacted records, often emphasizing problems retrospectively. The legal basis for a Phase 1 hold includes unreasonable significant risk, insufficient information to assess risk, an inadequate investigator brochure, and unqualified investigators. An unresolved efficacy-design disagreement is not automatically an initial Phase 1 hold criterion. [21 CFR 312.42](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-C/section-312.42).

## Implications for the guide

The lesson should culminate in a compact horizontal plan: **Question → Participants → Regimen and observations → Review and stopping rules → Next decision**. Keep dose calculation in the adjacent first-dose lesson. Here, ask whether every proposed measurement changes an interpretation or action, whether exclusions follow identifiable risks, and what finding would pause a participant, cohort, or study. Those stopping levels should be distinguished rather than collapsed into a single red button. A participant may require interruption while other participants continue; an emerging pattern may require pausing new enrollment or escalation; a broader problem may require reconsidering the study. These are proposed teaching distinctions, not thresholds supplied by these cases. The learner should identify the responsible reviewer, the observations needed before proceeding, and how a decision and its rationale would be documented.

A concept-only panel can show the team's deliverable: one bounded, interpretable human investigation. Separate examples can contrast oteseconazole with ensartinib, then use faricimab or olipudase for conditional progression and venetoclax for adaptation. Levacetylleucine and paltusotine are especially useful for exposing the difference between authorization and informative design. This structure connects the candidate's evidence to real protocol choices while preserving the uncertainty that makes the first study necessary.
