# Non-oncology evidence for “Connect drug action to potential benefit”

Reviewed 2026-09-16. Research only; no lesson or application edits. The companion JSON contains nine manually checked cases, exact FDA URLs and one-based PDF pages. Eight come from the original 34-record set; avacincaptad pegol was found by the broader cached-review screen and independently checked here. This is a targeted passage review, not a claim to have read every page of every approval package.

## What the cases change about the lesson

The evidence does not support one obligatory story of target → mechanism → biomarker-defined responders. The useful common task is narrower and more demanding: explain what this candidate does, which intended function that could change, and what observation would make the next development decision more credible. Different starting points call for different experiments.

**Established action can move the question forward without restarting discovery.** In the actual glycopyrrolate initial US IND, the reviewer already had prior approved uses and two UK COPD studies showing bronchodilation. The proposed study explored a repeated inhalation regimen and delivery system. In the actual cinacalcet original US IND, healthy-volunteer PTH and calcium responses already existed before the proposed patient study. Neither case makes “prove the target again” a sensible default. The lesson should first ask what is already known about the candidate, route, human exposure and intended function, then identify the remaining decision. The boundary between discovery and IND work is a change in the question, not erasure of the evidence.

**A physiological effect is not identical to patient benefit.** Cinacalcet provides unusually clear wording: suppressing parathyroid hormone is observed; improvement in renal bone disease is expected. The same intervention lowers calcium and can become undesirable. PTH can therefore be a useful disease-related and pharmacodynamic measurement without this passage establishing it as a complete substitute for clinically meaningful benefit. The learner should practice writing both claims: “we observed this response” and “we expect it to matter for this reason.” The distinction should remain visible even when the mechanism is familiar.

**A second target should earn its place through a comparison that tests its contribution.** Faricimab’s initial-IND studies progress from dual binding to a controlled monkey lesion experiment with anti-VEGF, anti-ANG-2 and negative comparators. Comparing equimolar anti-VEGF binding sites makes the functional question sharper than a catalogue of affinity values. The result supports greater reduction in lesion severity in that model, not clinical superiority, synergy, visual benefit or a patient ANG-2 selection cutoff. This could anchor a lesson exercise asking which inference is supported by binding alone, which needs the functional experiment, and which still requires human evidence.

**A well-understood disease defect does not eliminate delivery, timing and tissue questions.** Vestronidase has a straightforward enzyme-replacement rationale, but the actual IND excerpt still asks whether enzyme enters human disease-derived cells and remains active. The supporting mouse literature shows uneven tissue correction and differences between treatment from birth and one later dose. Crucially, those mouse experiments used recombinant murine enzyme; they must not all be relabeled as clinical-product studies. An enzyme-deficiency diagnosis is not the same scientific object as a predictive responder cutoff. The important remaining question may be whether the drug can reach and alter damage in the relevant compartment.

**“Mechanism unknown” must name the effect whose mechanism is unknown.** The later minocycline-foam IND review explicitly states no foam pharmacology studies were conducted in the material it describes and cites unknown acne mechanism/pharmacodynamics for the listed oral product. Later NDA work demonstrates antibacterial activity of the actual formulation, yet the microbiology reviewer still recommends that the acne mechanism be described as unknown. The pivotal trials use lesion and clinical-severity outcomes and have no clinical microbiology assessments. This is not absence of scientific work: it is a program in which known antibacterial action, a plausible but incomplete therapeutic explanation, local delivery and measured clinical outcomes coexist. Do not claim that there were no biomarkers of any kind, or backdate the later IND statement to the initial 2015 review.

**Action is contextual, including how it is measured.** Baricitinib’s NDA reviewer explicitly distinguishes selectivity in isolated JAK-enzyme assays from its absence in the human-leukocyte assays examined. The rodent efficacy experiment also used predicted exposure from another experiment rather than measuring plasma drug in the same animals. These are not merely technical footnotes: they determine what “selective” and “exposure-response” can legitimately mean. This is a supporting later-development example, not evidence of what the original IND required.

**A pharmacodynamic assay can be valuable without being a selection test.** The new avacincaptad case explicitly attributes functional complement studies to the initial IND. It combines binding specificity, classical/alternative pathway assays, species comparisons and systemic monkey concentration–response observations. The review also describes qualification of hemolysis assays for monitoring anti-C5 activity in clinical subjects. That is evidence for measuring drug action, not a validated pretreatment responder test. Systemic complement inhibition also does not establish that intravitreal therapy changes retinal degeneration. Keep the 2023 geographic-atrophy rationale separate from the exact, unreconstructed initial clinical plan.

**Potential benefit need not mean restoring a defective pathway.** Drospirenone’s original-IND appendix connects receptor affinity to ovulation inhibition and other hormone functions; contraception is an intended physiological intervention. Nitisinone offers another structure: block upstream metabolism rather than replace the defective downstream enzyme. Its explicitly IND-derived pathway diagram makes both the beneficial reduction of harmful products and the new tyrosine burden intelligible. The latter case is mixed-stage evidence: the diagram is attributed to the 1995 IND; the mechanistic rat summaries reviewed here are NDA-stage.

## Suggested construction choices, not implementation instructions

A lesson could open with three short contrasts: glycopyrrolate (established action, new delivery/regimen), faricimab (added action needs a functional comparator), and minocycline (clinical benefit can be investigated while its mechanism remains incomplete). Each gives the same learner question a different answer: what is the next uncertainty worth resolving?

The central exercise should require a short claim-and-evidence table rather than a comprehensive pathway drawing. Useful columns are: observation; experimental setting and comparator; supported inference; unresolved translation; next decision. A second brief exercise can label measurements by their actual role—disease definition, drug-action readout, safety monitoring, clinical outcome, or potential predictive selection—without requiring every program to populate every category.

Preserve the regulatory distinction requested by the user only where it belongs, in patient selection. The cases document sponsor choices and reviewer assessments; they do not create a universal FDA requirement. Enrollment rationale and criteria, program-specific safeguards, and any justified test needs remain separate from a blanket demand for a validated predictive biomarker. Strong scientific work is also not reducible to the minimum filing requirement: a sponsor may need better mechanistic or functional evidence to decide whether development is worthwhile even when its absence is not independently a Phase 1 hold reason.

## Stage and coverage audit

| Case | Best use | Stage boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Cinacalcet | Hormone response versus hoped-for benefit | Actual original US IND; previous human Phase 1, proposed patient Phase 2 |
| Drospirenone/ethinyl estradiol | Binding, physiological function, combination effects | Original-IND pharmacology appendix; animal function, not human efficacy |
| Vestronidase | Cellular uptake, persistence, tissue/timing limits | Actual IND excerpt; clinical-product studies versus murine-enzyme literature |
| Glycopyrrolate | Established action, new route/regimen | Actual original US IND after UK human studies; later-label pharmacology kept separate |
| Faricimab | Functional value of a second target | Studies explicitly attributed to initial IND; corrected data named in later BLA |
| Minocycline foam | Known action, incompletely explained therapeutic benefit | Later IND SD 36 plus separate NDA mechanistic/clinical evidence |
| Nitisinone | Upstream intervention and linked benefit/risk | IND-derived diagram; other checked mechanistic passages are NDA-stage |
| Baricitinib | Assay-context selectivity and uncertain exposure bridge | NDA action assessment; not verified as original-IND evidence |
| Avacincaptad pegol | Action measurements versus patient-selection tests | Explicit initial-IND studies summarized in NDA; systemic PD is not retinal efficacy |

The original 34-record metadata and prior nonclinical maps were screened for relevance before the passage selection. Existing safety/CMC-only anchors were not promoted into action-to-benefit cases merely because they mention an IND. The following decisions account for other non-oncology records:

- Suvorexant, arimoclomol, phenobarbital and fosphenytoin: CNS/uncertain-mechanism review assigned to the other agent. Existing suvorexant pharmacology mapping remains relevant; no duplicate case written here.
- Vardenafil: checked the retained Part 2 IND appendix; the substantive retained sections inspected concern chronic-study exposure and toxicity. Not promoted as candidate-action evidence without the relevant primary-pharmacology pages.
- Lenacapavir: actually read the mechanistic NDA pages 420–428 and regulatory history on 332. Excellent orthogonal assays, mutant controls, and an explicitly undetermined part of the late-stage mechanism; retained as a future supporting lead because these experiments were not individually tied to initial IND in the reviewed passage. Stronger stage-specific avacincaptad evidence replaced it in the nine-case set.
- Diclofenac/Pennsaid, ipratropium HFA and halobetasol: prior verified passages primarily answer new-route/formulation or human-bridging questions. These are useful elsewhere but less direct for this lesson than glycopyrrolate.
- Gadoxetate: the prior substantive IND appendix is reproductive/genetic toxicology; no action-to-diagnostic-benefit case inferred from it.
- Tofacitinib: the verified IND-derived receptor/channel table is principally secondary-pharmacology context; baricitinib offers a stronger explicit assay-selectivity comparison, with its later stage declared.
- Oteseconazole and Paxlovid: targeted text screening identified mechanistic NDA summaries, but the retained baseline IND anchors concern later clinical design or other development questions; not added merely to increase the count.
- Glucagon, chlorhexidine/isopropyl alcohol, budesonide oral suspension, and cord-blood records: retained baseline deficiency/bridging or development records do not supply the needed action experiments. No inference of mechanism sufficiency or biomarker absence.
- Oncology programs and investigational cell/gene programs assigned to the root/other review streams were not duplicated here. Clemidsogene’s baseline CRL is not a substantive pharmacology experiment.

This is a curated foundation, not a representative estimate of how commonly any evidence strategy is used across INDs. Public approval packages overrepresent surviving development programs and often combine decades of evidence. The companion JSON keeps those provenance limits explicit.
