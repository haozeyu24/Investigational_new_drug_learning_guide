# Justifying a first dose

Prepared 17 September 2026 for future lesson construction. Nine program examples are drawn from FDA public reviews and correspondence. They cover small molecules, an albumin-bound formulation, enzymes, and antibodies. Critical passages were reread; the accompanying evidence file preserves their provenance and limitations. This is a focused synthesis, not a complete inventory of FDA dose decisions or a dosing guide.

## Separate three decisions before introducing a calculation

The first dose, the next escalation, and the eventual therapeutic regimen answer different questions. The first is a prediction before adequate human data exist. Escalation uses emerging human findings within a justified operating plan. The selected regimen must ultimately balance activity, tolerability, duration, and practical administration. Confusing these decisions makes a reassuring starting-dose margin appear to justify an entire study.

The underlying inputs also differ. A dose is what is administered; exposure is what the organism experiences over time. An animal NOAEL is the highest tested dose without observed adverse effects in that study, not proof that nothing happens biologically or that all hazards have been discovered. A human-equivalent dose is an extrapolation. A safety factor addresses uncertainty in that extrapolation; it is not an experimentally measured probability that a person will avoid harm.

FDA's 2005 guidance describes an MRSD approach for adult healthy volunteers, primarily for intended systemic exposure: select a relevant animal NOAEL, derive a human-equivalent dose, choose the appropriate species, and apply a justified safety factor. It explicitly excludes escalation and maximum allowable trial doses from its scope. It also recommends considering pharmacologically active doses, especially when exaggerated pharmacology may cause toxicity. Its default factor of ten is a recommendation within this framework, not a universal law for all products or populations. [FDA MRSD guidance, PDF 4–7,12–15](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Estimating-the-Maximum-Safe-Starting-Dose-in-Initial-Clinical-Trials-for-Therapeutics-in-Adult-Healthy-Volunteers.pdf#page=4).

The enforceable protocol requirement is broader: describe the method of determining dose, planned maximum dosage, individual exposure duration, and risk-minimizing procedures. The specific calculation must be scientifically appropriate to the molecule, route, patient population, and prior knowledge. [21 CFR 312.23(a)(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.23).

## A large initial margin can coexist with an inadequate escalation rationale

The original **ivacaftor** IND review supplies an unusually direct example. Fourteen-day rat and dog studies found no target organ at the highest tested doses. The reviewer calculated a roughly 76–79-fold surface-area margin at the proposed 25 mg starting dose, but only about twofold at the proposed 800 mg maximum. Because the toxicity profile was insufficiently characterized to identify what to monitor, the reviewer accepted a modified 500 mg maximum and requested higher doses in future animal studies. The conclusion was not “no toxicity, therefore escalate freely.” It was that a limited first study could proceed with a narrower boundary. [FDA reproduced original IND review, PDF 122](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2012/203188Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=122).

**Faricimab** reaches a similar decision through a different hazard. Its initial IND assessment supported 0.5 mg per eye, but not the proposed 6 mg high dose, because severe ocular inflammation remained a concern. The sponsor's antibody-response explanation did not settle whether that finding was irrelevant to humans. FDA required review of lower-dose human findings before higher dosing. Local ocular administration also makes it inappropriate to copy a systemic body-surface-area calculation without considering local tissue exposure and injury. The review quotes original reasoning but has internally inconsistent calendar entries. [FDA pharmacology review, PDF 15,26](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761235Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=26).

These cases justify two separate boxes in the lesson: “Why begin here?” and “What would justify going higher?” The second must include observations and decision rules, not simply another multiplication factor.

## The biological reference point requires interpretation

**Avalglucosidase alfa** shows why readers must inspect the underlying review rather than copy a summary table. The reproduced July 2013 preliminary IND assessment identified 50 mg/kg as the monkey NOAEL, based on impaired weight gain at 200 mg/kg. It nevertheless considered 200 mg/kg tolerated, because findings were limited to small weight and organ-weight reductions without an identified target organ. Using that tolerated dose, it discussed a 13-fold margin for the proposed 5 mg/kg starting dose and 3.2-fold for 20 mg/kg. The same review also evaluated whether residual free glycan in the administered material was adequately qualified. Dose support depended on both biological interpretation and the actual product. [FDA integrated package, original review PDF 255,273–274](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/761194Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=273).

A later summary in that file calls 200 mg/kg the NOAEL and describes using both toxicology and glycogen-clearance pharmacology to select the starting dose. That inconsistency must remain visible: the original reviewer distinguished a NOAEL from a tolerated dose. It would be misleading to combine these passages into one apparently unanimous numerical derivation. [Later summary, PDF 35](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/761194Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=35).

For **pegzilarginase**, the retrospective first-in-human account combines monkey toxicology, exposure, and mechanism. A 0.015 mg/kg starting dose was related to a 0.3 mg/kg monkey NOAEL and a predicted AUC margin of roughly ten to eleven. Monkey arginine reduction provided additional pharmacological context. Transition to multiple doses then used the individual participant's PK and plasma arginine, without exceeding the highest dose already used in the single-dose portion. This distinguishes predicted initial exposure from a regimen subsequently informed by measured human response. The numerical margin is specific to this enzyme and study; it is not a universal acceptance threshold. [FDA integrated review, PDF 93,192](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2026/761211Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=93).

## Advanced oncology is a different clinical context

ICH S9 concerns patients with advanced cancer and limited therapeutic options. Its common small-molecule approaches include a fraction of the rodent severely toxic dose or the relevant nonrodent highest non-severely toxic dose. The guidance does not require a NOAEL in every oncology program and distinguishes starting-dose reasoning from escalation. These recommendations cannot be transferred unchanged to healthy volunteers, prevention studies, or cancer populations with substantially different benefit-risk circumstances. [FDA ICH S9, PDF 5,7,9,12](https://www.fda.gov/media/73161/download#page=9).

**Albumin-bound sirolimus, ABI-009**, provides an actual clinical bridge. The FDA package ties the original IND to the CA401 advanced-solid-tumor study and presents the applicant-described account that its 45 mg/m² weekly start was approximately one tenth of the rat maximum tolerated repeat dose. This was a formulation and route development program for an already known drug, not a wholly uncharacterized target. Later clinical experience changed the schedule: frequent missed third-week doses contributed to a two-weeks-on, one-week-off schedule. The review also contrasts tolerability in oncology with a different risk profile in pulmonary arterial hypertension. A nominal dose cannot be judged independently of indication and schedule. [FDA review, PDF 28,54](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/213312Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=54).

This example should not be rewritten as proof that the rat-derived start was risk-free. The account reports adverse events even at the lowest oncology dose; the later regimen required human evidence. Nor should its reference dose be silently relabeled STD10 when the cited dose-selection passage calls it a maximum tolerated repeat dose.

## Mechanistic and model-based predictions can supplement the toxicology anchor

For **glofitamab**, the applicant-described modeling work in the FDA review integrated monkey PK, cytokine responses, and in-vitro assays. A human starting-dose prediction aimed to keep IL-6 below a predefined threshold; the model table reports a proposed 5 µg first-human dose. Target-mediated disposition and species differences were relevant, and the source explicitly notes that rapid monkey clearance did not translate directly to humans. This is a retrospective description of a proposed first-human prediction, not an original FDA letter approving that number. Later IND discussions required additional hospitalization after cytokine-release events, demonstrating that a model did not replace clinical protection. [FDA multidisciplinary review, PDF 36–37,50–51](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/761309Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf#page=50).

MABEL means a dose expected to produce a minimal biological effect; it is not synonymous with an efficacious dose or a fixed percentage of receptor occupancy for every drug. FDA's June 2026 QSP/MABEL document discusses integrating human-cell pharmacology, relevant animal data, mechanistic models, and uncertainty analyses for products where this approach is appropriate. It remains **draft guidance, not for implementation**, and should appear in an optional current-methods note rather than as an established mandatory IND algorithm. [FDA draft, PDF 4–7,11–12](https://www.fda.gov/media/193230/download#page=4).

**Risdiplam** shows a different model-informed problem: starting treatment in infants after earlier adult exposure. The first infant in FIREFISH received a small single dose chosen using a conservative physiologically based PK scenario plus an additional safety factor. After evaluating measured PK, the team selected repeat dosing to target an exposure predicted to increase SMN protein; a further exposure level remained below the stated animal NOAEL exposure. Internal and independent committees reviewed safety, PK, and PD. This is first dosing in a vulnerable patient population, not first-ever human exposure to the molecule. The case makes uncertainty actionable: predict conservatively, measure, and revise before continuing. [FDA clinical review, PDF 24,72–74](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2020/213535Orig1s000MedR.pdf#page=73).

## Human findings change the permissible path

With **olipudase alfa**, the retrospective review reports an initial 0.03 mg/kg adult dose based on a tenfold factor relative to the disease-model mouse single-dose NOAEL. Higher single doses produced inflammatory reactions and hyperbilirubinemia. Subsequent development used gradual within-patient escalation to reduce accumulated sphingomyelin progressively rather than release its breakdown products too abruptly. Initial IND permissions themselves were staged: single-dose investigation first, then limited repeat-dose investigation while other work remained held. Starting, escalation, and maintenance were therefore separate scientific and regulatory decisions. [FDA integrated review, PDF 52–53,132,163](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761261Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=52).

**Venetoclax** provides a small-molecule oncology counterpoint. Its early starting dose had a nonclinical basis, yet tumor lysis occurred at initial dosing and during escalation. Amendments lowered the start and lengthened ramp-up while adding risk stratification, hydration, uric-acid management, hospitalization, and laboratory surveillance. These bundled changes prevent a simple causal claim that lowering the dose alone solved the problem. The lesson is that an accepted starting rationale is revisable when human evidence exposes a risk the original plan did not adequately control. [FDA clinical review, PDF 28,64,96](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2016/208573Orig1s000MedR.pdf#page=64).

## Implications for the guide

Use a horizontal reasoning sequence: **Clinical context → Relevant biological limits → Predicted human exposure → Proposed first dose → Conditions for escalation**. Under each, show the team's question and the evidence that changes its answer. The accompanying exercise should ask the learner to identify a missing assumption, an unsupported upper dose, or a need to revise a prediction after observed PK. It should not reward mechanical application of one conversion factor.

The most useful paired example is ivacaftor and faricimab: a supported first dose does not justify unrestricted escalation. Avalglucosidase teaches close reading of “NOAEL” versus “tolerated.” Risdiplam and pegzilarginase demonstrate the transition from model to measured human data. Glofitamab supplies a mechanistic contrast; olipudase and venetoclax demonstrate why the dose belongs inside a monitoring and adaptation plan. Public sources expose selected decisions, not every sponsor calculation, and their historical numbers should remain evidence for reasoning rather than reusable clinical recipes.
