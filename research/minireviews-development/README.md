# Eight focused reviews of IND-enabling development

*September 17, 2026 · Research for constructing Parts 3A, 3B and 3C*

The FDA records make these eight topics more concrete. Each concerns a different decision: interpreting exposure, interpreting harm, judging study adequacy, defining and controlling the product, preserving it through administration, designing an informative trial, and choosing how dosing can begin. The same experiment may contribute to several decisions, but it does not answer all of them.

These reviews extend [Connect drug action to potential benefit](../minireview-action/drug-action-potential-benefit.md). Each includes documented FDA cases, the stage at which the evidence was considered, limitations of the public record, regulatory context, and recommendations for the lesson. The website has not been changed as part of this review.

## 1. Connect dose, exposure, and effect

**A measured concentration becomes useful when its timing, biological meaning and comparability are clear.** The recurring problems are mismatched formulations, incomplete metabolite measurements, uninformative sampling and PD markers that show activity without distinguishing doses.

Vardenafil's later IND review requested parent and active-metabolite Cmax and AUC because a measurement at one clock time could not support the intended long-term exposure comparison. Fosdenopterin's retained assessment found that some disease-related markers did not discriminate doses under the tested conditions. These are two different limitations: insufficient observation of exposure and insufficient discrimination by the biological readout. Neither should be solved by simply adding more numbers to a table. [Vardenafil, PDF 16–17](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2003/21-400_Levitra_Pharmr_P2.pdf#page=16); [fosdenopterin, PDF 179–180](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/214018Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=179).

**Suggested lesson output:** an annotated exposure–effect comparison that identifies measured and predicted quantities, sampling limitations, and the human inference being proposed. Dose selection then uses this argument in Part 3C.

## 2. Identify and interpret potential harm

**An adverse finding needs an explanation of its conditions and consequences.** Organ affected, severity, onset, exposure, recovery and relevance to the proposed participants matter together. Absence of a finding is also bounded by what the experiment could detect.

Rilzabrutinib provides a particularly useful investigation of competing explanations. An initial IND hold involved dog neuroinflammation. A later IV comparison had lower exposure, limiting conclusions about whether the route explained the finding. Further work changed the assessment without establishing a simple, universal account of the mechanism. Lumateperone adds another boundary: human metabolites below the quantification limit narrowed a specific concern under tested conditions; they did not demonstrate that those metabolites could never form. The full review preserves these stages and uncertainties.

**Suggested lesson output:** a finding-to-consequence map: observation → competing explanations → discriminating evidence → implication for exposure, monitoring, eligibility or further investigation. This lesson interprets harm; the next judges whether the supporting studies are adequate.

## 3. Build a credible safety package

**Adequacy belongs to a proposed investigation.** A study can be well conducted yet poorly matched to the route, formulation, duration or population being proposed. Existing evidence is reusable when the applicability argument holds.

Glycopyrrolate inhalation's initial review accepted support for a bounded clinical study while retaining route-specific questions and report follow-up. Suvorexant's public history describes a formulation difference that complicated reuse of animal studies: initial support for a first trial did not settle all later needs. The sponsor's eventual choice to repeat extensive work should remain distinct from FDA's request for a justification of relevance. [Glycopyrrolate, PDF 31–33](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2017/208437Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=31); [suvorexant, PDF 34](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2014/204569Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=34).

**Suggested lesson output:** a trial-support matrix showing available evidence, applicability, design limitations, report status and the next decision gate. “Design accepted,” “study running” and “final report adequate” need separate states.

## 4. Define the drug people will receive

**The molecule is only part of the administered product.** Formulation, excipients, concentration, preparation, route and delivery device can change exposure or local effects. This lesson should establish what was tested and what will actually be given to participants.

The public ivacaftor review describes exposure comparisons involving materials and vehicles associated with clinical and nonclinical use, while important material identifiers remain redacted. That comparison supports a specific bridge; it does not disclose the complete clinical recipe. Atrovent HFA's later device history shows why changing a delivery component can reopen the connection to existing clinical evidence. [Ivacaftor, PDF 90](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2012/203188Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=90); [Atrovent HFA, PDF 38–39](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2004/021527s000_MedR.pdf#page=38).

**Suggested lesson output:** a product map linking discovery, nonclinical and clinical material, with each meaningful difference and its supporting evidence. The learner should be able to explain which conclusions survive a change and which need reconsideration.

## 5. Make and control consistent material

**A useful control has a clinically relevant purpose, a capable measurement and a justified acceptance rule.** More tests do not automatically create better control. A negative result means little without knowing what the method could detect.

Vamorolone's initial IND history records a partial hold over structural alerts in two impurities, followed by removal after genotoxicity studies were submitted. ACER-001 provides a contrasting decision: FDA distinguished an exploratory taste-mask integrity assay from routine QC while retaining expectations for appropriate coating controls and a discriminating dissolution method. One case required consequential evidence; the other clarified which test belonged to which decision. [Vamorolone, PDF 116](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/215239Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=116); [ACER-001, PDF 7–8](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/214860Orig1s000AdminCorres.pdf#page=7).

**Suggested lesson output:** a control rationale connecting failure mode, source of variation, method or preventive control, acceptance rule, patient-use basis and response to failure.

## 6. Keep the product suitable through dosing

**Quality must remain supported through the final administration step.** Unopened shelf life, time after preparation, container compatibility and delivery performance are different claims.

Lenacapavir's later IND holds involved inadequate information about injection risks associated with a vial-compatibility and particle problem. Cytalux's quality review links hypersensitivity during IND studies to saline dilution, with aggregation identified as the most likely explanation; preparation consequently used dextrose. These cases make stability tangible: a product can encounter new failure modes when stored in a particular container or prepared for use. [Lenacapavir, PDF 318–319](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/215973%2C215974Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=318); [Cytalux, PDF 6, 12](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/214907Orig1s000ChemR.pdf#page=6).

**Suggested lesson output:** a supported-use timeline from manufacture to administration, identifying conditions, elapsed time, plausible failures, supporting evidence and resulting instructions.

## 7. The first clinical study

**A protocol turns an evidence argument into decisions people must make at a clinical site.** Who enters, what is measured, when information is reviewed and what changes after an adverse event are central to trial design.

The cases include genetically focused and broader enrollment strategies, limited exploratory studies, washout questions, staged cohorts and restrictions affecting only one portion of a combined-phase protocol. Faricimab illustrates a request to review lower-dose human experience before higher dosing. Futibatinib demonstrates why clearance for one part of a program should not be treated as clearance for every proposed cohort. The review also distinguishes an initial US IND from first-ever human administration and labels biologic comparisons.

**Suggested lesson output:** a trial decision map linking participants, objectives, measurements, review gates and actions. It should show what can be learned at this stage and what the trial cannot establish about later benefit.

## 8. Justifying a first dose

**Starting, escalating and selecting a later regimen are separate decisions.** A defensible initial dose cannot carry the entire escalation plan by itself. Reference point, exposure metric, species relevance, uncertainty and clinical controls must stay attached to the calculation.

Ivacaftor's original review supported the proposed low starting dose while questioning the planned upper dose and recommending a lower ceiling. Avalglucosidase adds an unusually important source-reading lesson: a later summary and the reproduced IND assessment use different NOAEL/tolerated-dose descriptions. The review preserves that disagreement rather than silently selecting the more convenient value. [Ivacaftor, PDF 122](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2012/203188Orig1s000PharmR.pdf#page=122); [avalglucosidase, original assessment PDF 273](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/761194Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=273) and [later summary PDF 35](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2021/761194Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf#page=35).

**Suggested lesson output:** a dose argument showing the reference observation, translation method, uncertainty allowance, starting dose, escalation conditions and evidence needed before the next increase. The adult-healthy-volunteer MRSD framework, oncology approaches and mechanism-based approaches need their proper scopes. The June 2026 QSP/MABEL document remains draft guidance.

## What this changes about the overall teaching plan

The nine topics, including drug action and potential benefit, form a connected argument:

**Potential benefit → informative exposure → understood hazards → adequate supporting evidence.**

**Defined product → controlled variation → supported use through administration.**

**Those two streams inform a clinical study and a dose plan that generate the next evidence.**

This is a teaching organization, not a fixed sequence of departmental handoffs. A formulation change can reopen an exposure comparison. A new adverse finding can alter eligibility or escalation. A clinical administration problem can send work back to CMC.

The most useful common display for the website would be **evidence → interpretation → decision → remaining uncertainty**, with an unobtrusive source-stage label on every historical example. This provides room for scientific judgment while preventing a later marketing requirement from becoming an invented prerequisite for every initial IND.

## Coverage and source files

The automated search examined available extracted text from a frozen corpus of **7,979 FDA PDF URLs**. It identified **2,097 candidate documents across overlapping topic queues**. Manual close reading was a bounded selection, expanded by earlier verified records and targeted FDA retrieval. This is a broad search followed by focused reviews, not an exhaustive manual review of every FDA document.

The retained reviews contain **85 topic assignments across 51 development programs**, citing **52 FDA PDF URLs and 157 distinct document-pages**. Programs recur where they illustrate different decisions. The broader manual audit, including triage, records **178 pages across 64 PDFs**; this does not mean those PDFs were read in full.
